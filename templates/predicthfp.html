Heart Failure Prediction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the number 1 cause of death globally, taking an estimated 17.9 million lives each year, which accounts for 31% of all deaths worlwide.

Heart failure is a common event caused by CVDs and this dataset contains 12 features that can be used to predict mortality by heart failure.


Most cardiovascular diseases can be prevented by addressing behavioural risk factors such as tobacco use, unhealthy diet and obesity, physical inactivity and harmful use of alcohol using population-wide strategies.

People with cardiovascular disease or who are at high cardiovascular risk (due to the presence of one or more risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidaemia or already established disease) need early detection and management wherein a machine learning model can be of great help.


About This Dataset:

Dataset from Davide Chicco, Giuseppe Jurman: Machine learning can predict survival of patients with heart failure from serum creatinine and ejection fraction alone. BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making.

Handling Class Imbalance 

  smote = SMOTE(random_state=42)

Medical datasets often suffer from class imbalance, where one class significantly outnumbers the other. In this project, the target variable (DEATH_EVENT) contains fewer positive cases compared to negative cases, which can bias machine learning models.


To address this issue, SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) is applied.


Technique Used: SMOTE

SMOTE works by generating synthetic samples for the minority class rather than duplicating existing records. This helps create a more balanced dataset and improves model learning.


How SMOTE Is Applied:
  • SMOTE is applied only to the training data
  • Synthetic samples are generated based on nearest neighbors
  • The test data remains untouched to ensure fair evaluation

Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves are used to visually compare the performance of different machine learning models. ROC analysis is especially important for imbalanced medical datasets, where accuracy alone may be misleading.


What the ROC Curve Represents:
  • True Positive Rate (TPR): Sensitivity or recall
  • False Positive Rate (FPR): Probability of false alarms

Each ROC curve shows how well a model distinguishes between:

  • Survived patients (0)
  • Deceased patients (1)

ROC Curve Generation:
  • ROC curves are plotted for all trained models using predicted probabilities.
  • Each curve represents a different machine learning model.
  • A diagonal reference line represents random guessing.

Model Comparison:
  • Models closer to the top-left corner indicate better performance.
  • Curves farther from the diagonal line show stronger classification ability.
  • The ROC-AUC score quantifies the overall performance of each model.

Heart Failure Prediction – Input Details

Age:

  • Description: Age of the patient in years.
  • Why it matters: Risk of heart failure generally increases with age.

Anaemia (Yes / No):

  • Description: Indicates whether the patient has anaemia (low red blood cell count).
  • Why it matters: Anaemia can reduce oxygen supply to the heart and worsen heart conditions.

Creatinine Phosphokinase (CPK):

  • Description: Level of CPK enzyme in the blood (mcg/L).
  • Why it matters: High levels may indicate heart muscle damage.

Diabetes (Yes / No):

  • Description: Indicates whether the patient has diabetes.
  • Why it matters: Diabetes increases the risk of cardiovascular diseases.

Ejection Fraction:

  • Description: Percentage of blood pumped out of the heart during each contraction.
  • Why it matters: Lower values indicate poor heart pumping efficiency.

High Blood Pressure (Yes / No):

  • Description: Indicates whether the patient has hypertension.
  • Why it matters: High blood pressure puts extra strain on the heart.

Platelets:

  • Description: Platelet count in the blood (kiloplatelets/mL).
  • Why it matters: Abnormal platelet levels can affect blood clotting and heart health.

Serum Creatinine:

  • Description: Level of creatinine in the blood (mg/dL).
  • Why it matters: Reflects kidney function, which is closely linked to heart health.

Serum Sodium:

  • Description: Sodium level in the blood (mEq/L).
  • Why it matters: Low sodium levels are associated with worse heart failure outcomes.

Gender (Male / Female):

  • Description: Biological sex of the patient.
  • Why it matters: Heart disease risk and symptoms can vary by gender.

Smoking (Yes / No):

  • Description: Indicates whether the patient is a smoker.
  • Why it matters: Smoking significantly increases cardiovascular risk.

Follow-up Period (Days):

  • Description: Number of days the patient was monitored.
  • Why it matters: Used to evaluate survival and outcomes over time.
